## Part 1 — Quantitative Background (Weeks 1–2)

Building statistical intuition from first principles before any formal
machinery: sampling variability and inference by hand (Lab 1), then
translating biological questions into explicit mathematical models — response
variables, parameters, and error structure (Lab 2).

## Part 2 — Quantifying Uncertainty (Weeks 3–4)

Two complementary routes to a p-value: theoretical distribution functions
(*t*, *F*, χ², Normal — Lab 3) and empirical randomization / Monte Carlo
reshuffling (Lab 4). Establishes that "the p-value" is a computed quantity,
not a lookup.

## Part 3 — The General Linear Model: Single Explanatory Variable (Weeks 5–7)

Simple regression fit two ways — by least squares (Lab 5a) and verified by
randomization of the null slope (Lab 5b) — followed by single-factor ANOVA as
the categorical-predictor case of the same linear model (Lab 6a/b).

## Part 4 — The General Linear Model: Multiple Explanatory Variables (Weeks 8–9)

Factorial designs with interaction terms (Lab 7), then ANCOVA combining a
continuous covariate with a categorical predictor, including the
homogeneity-of-slopes assumption check (Lab 8).

## Parts 3–5 — Applied Integration (Weeks 10–11)

A two-part problem-solving sequence: formulating a research question into a
verifiable model structure (Lab 9a), then executing the full pipeline — data
cleaning, fitting, selection, diagnostics, and biological interpretation
(Lab 9b). This pair is this course's own applied-synthesis lab, drawing on
Parts 3–5 together, rather than a numbered part on Schneider's site.

## Part 5 — The Generalized Linear Model (Week 12)

Extending beyond Gaussian errors to binary/proportional outcomes via logistic
regression and the logit link (Lab 10).

## Part 6 — Extensions of the General Linear Model (Week 13)

Resampling-based (bootstrap) estimation of standard errors and confidence
intervals, closing the loop back to Part 2's empirical approach to
uncertainty (Lab 11).
